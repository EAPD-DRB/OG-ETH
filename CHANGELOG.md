## [0.3.0] - 2026-09-10 20:00:00

### Changed
* Recalibrated the lifetime earnings profiles `e` with Ethiopian data instead of a one-number tilt of the OG-USA profiles, implementing the two-step reshaping first proposed for OG-ZAF (EAPD-DRB/OG-ZAF#18, #63) as a re-runnable function. Over age, the OG-USA matrix is multiplied by the Ethiopia-to-US ratio of National Transfer Accounts labor income per worker (the 2005 and 2006 per-capita profiles divided by the ILOSTAT employment ratio of the same year), so Ethiopian earnings peak in the late thirties and fall to about half the US relative level after 60: the age profile of `e` relative to the thirties goes from 0.65/1.32/1.50/1.49 (twenties/forties/fifties/sixties, the US shape) to 0.69/1.21/1.06/0.72. Across lifetime-income groups, each group's profile is scaled so that the income shares the matrix implies equal the World Inequality Database's 2021 pre-tax income shares for Ethiopia (4.4, 13.0, 16.0, 11.0, 15.2, 30.0 and 10.5 percent); the lifetime means of the seven groups are 0.18, 0.52, 0.80, 1.10, 1.52, 3.33 and 10.47, and the Gini of `e` over the model population is 0.520 against WID's 0.516 (the previous matrix had a Gini of 0.36 and a top percent at 8.8 times the bottom quarter; it is now 59 times). `get_e_interp` also accepts other target shares (`group_shares`) or a single Gini coefficient (`gini_to_match`) in place of the WID shares. This closes #33: the previous calibration matched a World Bank *consumption* Gini for Ethiopia (31.1) against an *income* Gini for the United States (41.5). The inputs ship as small CSV files in `ogeth/data/` and `python -m ogeth.income` regenerates the packaged matrix; `e` is scaled on the model's own joint age-by-group population, and `Calibration` no longer requests a second set of 80-period demographics for it. `tests/test_income.py` covers the reshaping and pins the packaged matrix (#39).
* Calibrated the age profile of the disutility of labor `chi_n` to Ethiopian hours, instead of shipping OG-USA's eighty values as every OG-Core country model has done. The targets are hours worked per week per person by age from the 2021 Labour Force and Migration Survey (employment-to-population ratio times mean weekly hours of the employed), measured as OG-USA does against a 16-hour day, seven days a week, and tapered beyond the survey's open 65+ band with the NTA labor-income profile. `ogeth/labor.py` (rewritten from orphaned South African survey code) solves the steady state repeatedly, rescaling `chi_n` between solves by the ratio of marginal disutilities at the model's and the target's labor supply; from the OG-USA profile it converges in six solves to within 1.7 percent at every age from 20 to 79. Households in the model had been working about 46 percent of their time at every prime age, twice the survey's hours; they now work 19 hours a week per person at 20–24, 25 at 30–44, 20 in the fifties, 16 at 60–64 and 10 at 65–69. The weights rise from 20–25 (US) to 300–500 in the prime ages and reach OG-Core's cap of 10,000 around age 80. `python -m ogeth.labor` regenerates the profile and the steady-state initial guesses; the broken `ogeth/test_est_chi_n.py` driver is removed and `tests/test_labor.py` covers the targets and the update rule (closes the `chi_n` half of #71).
* Let fertility and mortality differ by lifetime-income group, using the income gradients OG-Core 0.18 accepts (`fert_gradient`, `infmort_gradient`, `mort_gradient`) with Ethiopia's own measurements from the [EAPD-DRB/Demographic-Gradients](https://github.com/EAPD-DRB/Demographic-Gradients) library, shipped in `ogeth/data/demographic_gradients_ETH.csv` and read by `ogeth.calibrate.demographic_gradients`. Fertility and infant mortality come from the 2024 Demographic and Health Survey by wealth quintile (tilts of −0.74 and −0.66 in the log rate from the poorest to the richest household; the poorest quintile has 5.7 children against 2.9 for the richest), and adult mortality from the household-deaths module of the 2007 census (−0.87 at ages 15–29, flat at 30–44); the census bands from 45 on measure higher mortality among the wealthy, which the library flags as at least partly a reporting artefact, so no tilt is applied there. OG-Core preserves the UN aggregate rates, so the population path and `g_n` are unchanged; the joint age-by-group distribution shifts a little (the poorest quarter of births is 24.5 percent of the adult population, the richest percent 1.02) and the poorest group's chance of surviving from 20 to 65 is 73.1 percent against 74.2 for the richest. The earnings matrix, the remittance and transfer allocations and the fiscal program paths are rebuilt on the new demographics; `python -m ogeth.update_baseline --demographics-only` does that from the UN data in place, leaving the hand-calibrated parameters in the file untouched (the full `update_baseline` still refreshes the macro parameters from the APIs, which would overwrite the calibrated capital share). `Calibration` now uses a named `UN_COUNTRY_CODE` constant and `tests/test_demographic_gradients.py` checks the gradients and the call into OG-Core.
* Let the adult-mortality gradient steepen with income over the transition. The Demographic-Gradients library finds that the gradient tracks national income across its censuses (tilt = 1.291 − 0.244 ln GNI per capita, r = −0.88), and Ethiopia's census measurement is from 2007, when GNI per head was $210 against $1,100 today; the measured tilts at ages 15–59 are therefore shifted by the rule's change since 2007 (about −0.41 in 2025) and keep steepening along the model's per-capita growth until income per head reaches the $10,000 where the rule's fit ends. OG-Core accepts the gradient as a (T+S, E+S) path, so `mort_gradient` is now such a path. The poorest quarter's mortality at 30 is 1.35 times the richest percent's in 2025 and 2.1 times in the long run; survival from 20 to 65 is 70 against 77 percent today and 67 against 80 percent in the long run.
* Replaced the flat income-tax rates with Ethiopia's statutory schedules. The tax functions are now OG-Core's Gouveia–Strauss form (`tax_func_type = "GS"`) fitted to the monthly employment-income brackets: Proclamation 979/2016 (600 birr exempt, 10–35 percent) in FY2024/25, with thresholds scaled into FY2025/26 birr, and Proclamation 1395/2025 (2,000 birr exempt, 15–35 percent) from FY2025/26 on; the marginal rate on capital income is the flat 10 percent dividend rate. `mean_income_data` moves from GNI per capita converted at a 2025 exchange rate (157,845 birr) to GDP per adult in FY2025/26 birr (353,050 birr), the concept OG-Core's `factor` needs. Under the statutory schedule the two formal groups face effective rates of 33–34 percent, so the compliance vector's *level* (`COMPLIANCE_SCALE`, calibrated to 0.204: the top percent remits a fifth of what Schedule A implies on its income, the sixth group a tenth) becomes the parameter that puts personal income tax at 1.42 percent of GDP in FY2024/25, with the same formalization path as before scaled with it; the effective income-tax rate is then about 3 percent for the sixth group and 7 percent for the top. Fitting code and schedules are in `ogeth/macro_params.py`; `tests/test_taxes_pensions_wealth.py` checks the fit against the brackets.
* Turned on the public pension as a defined-benefit scheme with Ethiopia's rules (Proclamations 1267/2022 and 1268/2022): retirement at 60, a benefit of 30 percent of the last three years' average salary plus 1.25 percent per year of service beyond ten, capped at 70 percent, which OG-Core's `Defined Benefits` system represents with `yr_contrib = 40`, `avg_earn_num_years = 3` and `alpha_db = 0.016875` (67.5 percent for a full career). Only the two formal groups are covered (`replacement_rate_adjust`), and the coverage vector is scaled (`PENSION_COVERAGE_SCALE`, calibrated to 0.87) so that pension outlays are 0.5 percent of GDP in FY2024/25, the IMF's figure (they rise to 0.8 percent in the steady state as the population ages) — the formal groups earn 40 percent of labor income in the model while the schemes have about 800,000 pensioners and cover under two percent of the working-age population. Pension payouts leave the cash-transfer ratio (`alpha_T` is 0.5 points of GDP lower along the program) and the transfer allocation `eta` no longer carries a pension slice; the payroll tax stays at its 3 percent effective rate.
* Calibrated the discount factor by lifetime-income group to the distribution of wealth, as OG-USA does. `beta_annual`, previously 0.88 for every group, is now 0.525, 0.525, 0.775, 0.852, 0.882, 0.909 and 0.967 by lifetime-income group, set so that each group's share of household wealth in the history-anchored baseline's steady state matches WID's net personal wealth shares for Ethiopia (2021) to within 3.5 percent: 4.8 percent for the bottom half, 37 percent for the middle 40, 58 percent for the top decile and 23.6 percent for the top percent (the bottom quarter's share is negative in the data and is given a 0.5 percent floor, since the model's households cannot borrow), while the level of the vector keeps steady-state household wealth at the 2.15-times-GDP anchor (2.16 in the solved steady state). The bottom half's 0.525 — a discount rate near 90 percent — is in the range Yesuf and Bluffstone measured experimentally among poor Ethiopian farm households (median annual rates of 43 to 105 percent). `chi_b` stays at OG-USA's 80 for every group. The WID wealth rows ship in `ogeth/data/wid_pretax_income_2021.csv`.
* Made the packaged baseline **history-anchored** and kept the IMF program as a scenario. Every fiscal and external input of the default parameters now takes its FY2024/25 outturn or enacted FY2025/26 budget value and is held there — spending ratios and grants (FY2025/26 budget), the effective consumption-tax rate, corporate-tax collections factor and income-tax compliance (FY2024/25), the foreign share of new borrowing (0.15 throughout), remittances (5.6 percent of GDP, flat) — and the long-run debt ratio is the measured 50.5 percent rather than the program's 30; the real rate on debt holds the value the FY2025/26 budget implies (−3.5 percent) for the program horizon before converging to 2 percent. Debt, growth and the external balance are model outcomes, compared with the IMF projections in the macro chapter but not fitted to them: with nothing imposed after the FY2025/26 budget, debt falls from 50.5 to 34.9 percent of GDP by FY2030/31 against the program's 28.6, so about a quarter of the program's debt decline is attributable to its measures and the rest to growth and the negative real rate on the legacy stock; the program scenario lands at 29.6 with the same household calibration. The program calibration is unchanged as a scenario: `macro_params.fiscal_program_params` now carries its own long-run debt ratio and premium centering, `scenario_params(p, scenario)` builds either set, and `examples/run_og_eth.py --scenario program` overlays it and writes its results under `examples/OG-ETH-Example/program`. The household-side parameters set by `python -m ogeth.calibrate` were re-matched on the history-anchored steady state. Tests cover both scenarios.
* Let the remittance share of GDP follow the IMF program in the program scenario instead of holding it at the FY2024/25 level. `alpha_RM_T` is now 0.041, the program's FY2030/31 projection, held for the long run like the other program series, and `g_RM` (`macro_params.program_share_g_RM`) scales the on-trend growth path by the ratio of consecutive shares so that RM/Y glides geometrically from the measured 5.6 percent of GDP to 4.1 over the program years and then stays there (the IMF's year-by-year path, which peaks at 6.0 percent and then falls by up to 0.6 points a year, would need remittance growth rates outside the bounds OG-Core places on `g_RM`). The validation figure and table now report the model's current account — net exports less the returns paid on foreign-owned capital and foreign-held debt, plus remittances and grants — which runs from −1.0 to −2.6 percent of GDP against the program's −1.1 to −2.7. The trade balance itself stays 5 points of GDP smaller than the data's, and the macro chapter explains why: OG-Core pays the household portfolio return on foreign-held public debt and foreign-owned capital, so factor payments abroad are about 5.7 percent of GDP against Ethiopia's roughly 1 percent of mostly concessional interest; with the same current account the model imports correspondingly less.
* Set the example's solver options to a damped outer loop with Anderson acceleration of the time path (`nu = 0.2`, `TPI_outer_method = "anderson"` where the installed OG-Core has it; `macro_params.NU`, `TPI_ANDERSON_M`, `TPI_ANDERSON_BETA`): with discount factors that differ by group the plain functional iteration needed twice as many transition iterations as before. The reform steady state starts from the baseline solution again (`reform_use_baseline_solution` true), which solves in seconds where the sweep over the packaged guesses did not.
* Added `python -m ogeth.calibrate`, which solves the steady state repeatedly and sets together the parameters whose targets are steady-state moments — `chi_n` (LFMS hours), `beta_annual` by group (WID wealth shares and the wealth anchor), the pension coverage scale (outlays 0.5 percent of GDP) and the compliance scale (personal income tax 1.4 percent of GDP in the first period) — and writes them into the packaged JSON.
* Re-anchored the parameters that depend on the earnings and labor profiles. The compliant-group income-tax rate `etr_params` moves from 0.1313 to 0.0871 so that personal income tax still collects 1.4 percent of GDP in the first model period (the two compliant groups now earn 40 percent of labor income, against 26 under the US-shaped profiles); the sovereign-rate shift path is re-evaluated at the new steady-state return on capital (7.9 percent, from 7.6); and the steady-state initial guesses are those of the calibrated solve. With all of the above the full example runs cleanly (baseline and corporate-tax-cut reform, steady-state resource-constraint errors below 1e-14): the debt ratio follows the program to within 1.4 points of GDP in every year and lands at 29.9 percent in FY2030/31 (28.6 in the program), tax revenue goes from 9.1 to 11.8 percent of GDP, personal income tax from 1.4 to 2.3 as compliance broadens, the steady-state return on capital is 7.7 percent with private capital at 2.2 times GDP and household wealth at 2.20, and the corporate-tax cut from 30 to 25 percent raises long-run GDP by 0.28 percent and the capital stock by 1.2 percent. The validation table in `macro.md` is refreshed to this run.
* Made **informality the baseline tax calibration**, modeling it as graded income-tax *non-compliance* by lifetime-income group rather than a flat blended rate. Ethiopia's ~85% informal employment (ILO 2021) was previously approximated by a single low effective rate on everyone, which imposed a spurious work-and-saving wedge on the ~90% of households that in fact remit no income tax. Using OG-Core's by-`(t,j)` noncompliance dials, the bottom five of seven lifetime-income groups (90% of households by population weight) are set fully non-compliant, group 6 half, and the top group fully compliant. Five parameters change in `ogeth_default_parameters.json`: `etr_params` 0.03 → **0.1313** (the compliant-group effective rate, solved from a revenue identity to hit the PIT anchor), `mtrx_params` 0.20 → **0.35** (Ethiopia's statutory top PIT rate), `labor_income_tax_noncompliance_rate` and `capital_income_tax_noncompliance_rate` both `[0,…,0]` → **`[1,1,1,1,1,0.5,0]`**, and `adjustment_factor_for_cit_receipts` 0.2 → **0.327** (re-anchored to actual corporate-tax collections); `income_tax_filer` stays all 1.0 (non-compliance, not non-filing). Steady-state revenue now matches the data instrument by instrument — PIT 1.39% of GDP (target 1.4), CIT 1.71% (1.7), direct taxes 3.10% (3.1); sources IMF SIP 2025/108 ¶9 and CR 26/20 Table 2b — with the correct incidence (average income-tax rate by group `[0,0,0,0,0,0.066,0.131]`). Removing the flat-rate wedge lifts steady-state output ~7% (a recalibration effect, not a welfare claim). Documented in `calibration/taxes.md`; refs #71. Lifetime income is a proxy for formality (high-income informality is not representable), and time-varying formalization *reforms* are a separate exercise.
* Recalibrated the real and fiscal blocks to bring the steady state substantially closer to the actual Ethiopian economy. (1) **Capital share**: `gamma` 0.515 → 0.30, replacing the naive ILOSTAT labour share (0.385, biased down by Ethiopia's pervasive self-employment) with a Gollin-adjusted / sectorally-weighted labour share of 0.60; this cuts the model capital-output ratio from ~3.5 toward the Penn World Table's ~2.2 and the foreign-owned capital stock from ~1.3× to ~0.29× GDP (against a ~0.24× FDI stock). (2) **Remittances turned on**: `alpha_RM` 0 → 0.056 (IMF CR 26/20 balance-of-payments net private transfers, ~5.6% of GDP), which flips the steady-state trade balance from a wrong-signed +5.7% surplus to a −6.8% deficit, matching Ethiopia's actual −6.9% goods-trade deficit. (3) **Foreign aid turned on**: `alpha_FA` 0 → 0.01 (a modest long-run grant level; World Bank DPO on-budget grants were ~1.7% of GDP in FY2024/25, declining per the IMF program; superseded by the program path below), routing grants to the government so it can fund its spending — Ethiopia's low domestic revenue (~9% of GDP) cannot otherwise support positive government consumption on a debt-stable path. (4) **Payroll tax**: `tau_payroll` 0.18 → 0.03, the coverage-weighted *effective* pension-contribution rate (the 18% statutory rate covers only the small formal sector), correcting tax/GDP from an implausible 13% to ~9.2% (matching general-government revenue excluding grants). (5) `alpha_I` set to Ethiopia's actual on-budget public investment (~0.05, from the aspirational 0.065 recovery path). (6) `initial_Kg_ratio` 0.50 → **0.67**, Ethiopia's measured public-capital stock (IMF ICSD, 2019), so the model starts from the real state/SOE-built infrastructure and lets it depreciate toward the sustainable steady state (~0.28) over the transition — the honest counterfactual for an over-built stock that current revenue cannot maintain. This lifts baseline GDP ~10% in the start year (fading over a generation) versus starting near the steady state; the steady state itself is unchanged (initial_Kg_ratio is an initial condition only). `initial_guess_r_SS` retuned to 0.052. Fiscal aggregates are the IMF/World Bank general-government figures (federal + regional consolidated); the federal-to-regional block grant is excluded from `alpha_T`.
* Refreshed the single-industry baseline calibration to Ethiopian FY2024/25 (model start 2025), the first full-year vintage after the July 2024 exchange-rate float. Macro and open-economy block: `initial_debt_ratio` 0.327 → 0.50 and `initial_foreign_debt_ratio` 0.42 → 0.63 (the float revalued external debt; IMF fourth-ECF-review DSA / MoF Debt Bulletin No. 56, end-June 2025), `debt_ratio_ss` 0.40 → 0.30 (IMF fifth-review medium-term path to ~28.6% by FY2030/31), `zeta_D` 0.12 → 0.15, `zeta_K` 0.20 → 0.16 (Ethiopia's normalized Chinn-Ito index, 0.162, cross-checked against FDI/GFCF), `alpha_G` 0.055 → 0.058 (WDI `NE.CON.GOVT.ZS`, 2025), `alpha_T` 0.05 → 0.04, `alpha_I` path raised to 0.05 → 0.065 and `initial_Kg_ratio` 0.40 → 0.50 (IMF public-investment path), `tau_c` 0.07 → 0.06 (effective consumption-tax rate, FY2024/25 indirect taxes over private consumption). `world_int_rate_annual` stays 0.04 (Ethiopia is a distressed, near-closed sovereign; its integration is captured through low `zeta_K` and the debt-elastic rate).
* Moved the `g_y_annual` growth window from 2006-2024 to **2016-2025** (`G_Y_START_YEAR`/`G_Y_END_YEAR`), refreshing `g_y_annual` 0.0595 → 0.0470. The longer window embedded the unrepeatable 2004-2015 investment boom; the post-2015 decade is a more defensible balanced-growth-path rate. `gamma` refreshed 0.5169 → 0.51475 (latest ILOSTAT labour-share vintage, 2025).
* Turned on a **centered debt-elastic sovereign premium** (`r_gov_DY = -0.024`, `r_gov_DY2 = 0.04`), following OG-PHL/OG-IDN. The premium `r_gov_DY2·(D/Y − debt_ratio_ss)²` is re-centered on the steady-state debt ratio (0.30) so it is exactly zero at target — the steady state is unchanged — and only prices the transition-path debt overshoot. `r_gov_shift` is re-centered accordingly (-0.03377 → -0.03737).
* Froze the `r_gov_*` parameters in the live `update_from_api` path (they are no longer returned by `get_macro_params`): the committed `r_gov_shift` is the re-centered value, and returning the raw Li-Magud-Werner shift would silently un-center the premium and move the steady state. The Li-Magud-Werner derivation plus the re-centering arithmetic is preserved as the reproducible `macro_params.estimate_r_gov` helper.
* Retuned `initial_guess_r_SS` (0.0648 → 0.093) to the refreshed steady state.
* Calibrated the transition path to the IMF program (Country Report 26/174, fifth ECF review, Tables 1, 2b and 4b) instead of holding FY2024/25 spending ratios fixed for twenty years. Previously the baseline ran a primary deficit of 5.6–6.1 percent of GDP for its first two decades (government consumption 5.8, transfers 4.0 and public investment 5.0 percent of GDP against 8.7 percent of revenue), debt climbed from 50 to 84 percent of GDP by year 10, and the closure rule later pushed government consumption below zero. The fiscal block now follows the program for FY2024/25–FY2030/31 (model periods 0–6) and holds the last value: government consumption (`alpha_G`, recurrent spending net of interest and cash transfers) 4.3 → 5.9 percent of GDP, cash transfers (`alpha_T`: fuel and fertilizer subsidies, the safety net, public pensions) 2.0 → 1.5 percent, public investment (`alpha_I`, capital expenditure) 5.0 → 5.1 percent, grants (`alpha_FA`, now a path) 1.7 → 0.3 percent, and revenue rising with the program from 9.2 to 12.3 percent of GDP through formalization (below), the consumption-tax rate (`tau_c` 0.060 → 0.078) and CIT collections (`adjustment_factor_for_cit_receipts` 0.327 → 0.472). `initial_debt_ratio` is 0.505 (the program's FY2024/25 figure) and the closure rule starts after the program (`tG1` 20 → 7). Reproducing the program's debt decline with primary balances near zero requires a negative real effective interest rate on the legacy debt — the counterpart of inflation and concessional terms eroding it — so `r_gov_shift` is a path that puts the sovereign rate on the program-implied real rates (−3.5 to −1.4 percent) and converges over four periods to a long-run 2 percent (the previous 4.5 percent long-run rate exceeded even the program's FY2030/31 nominal effective rate of 5.2 percent). OG-Core clips the sovereign rate at `r_gov_floor` (a parameter since PSLmodels/OG-Core#1203, hard-coded at zero before), and the example lowers the floor to −10 percent where the installed OG-Core has the parameter so the negative rates bind. The centering constant of the debt-elastic premium is now subtracted from the shift, as OG-Core's formula requires (`r_gov_DY d + r_gov_DY2 d² = r_gov_DY2 (d − D̄)² − r_gov_DY2 D̄²`); the previous calibration added it, which put the long-run sovereign rate about 0.7 percentage points below the level it documented. On an OG-Core without the parameter the rate sits at zero through the program and the debt ratio declines more slowly than the program's — to about 36 rather than 29 percent of GDP by FY2030/31 — reaching the 30 percent target through the closure rule afterwards; the gap is the inflation-tax channel a real model with a non-negative rate leaves out.
* Targeted cash transfers by programme instead of spreading them population-proportionally: the `eta` allocation matrix now gives the Productive Safety Net Program (0.4 percent of GDP) to the poorest quarter of households, the fertilizer subsidy (0.6 percent) per capita to the bottom 70 percent — smallholder agriculture — and public pension payouts (0.5 percent) to retired households in the two formal groups, so the fourth and fifth lifetime-income groups receive nothing and the poorest quarter receives 41 percent of transfers rather than 25. The matrix is rebuilt from the demographics by `update_baseline.py` and `Calibration`.
* Let the foreign share of net government borrowing follow the program: `zeta_D` is a path equal to the external creditors' share of each program year's debt decline in the IMF external/domestic split (0.37, 0.62, 0.69, 1.0, 0.96, 0.77 for FY2025/26–FY2030/31; CR 26/174, Table 1), returning to the calibrated 0.15 afterwards. With the constant 0.15 the model's external debt fell to 20 percent of GDP by FY2030/31 against the program's 16.7 because only 15 percent of each year's debt reduction was external.
* Lowered `beta_annual` further, 0.90 → 0.88, once the public capital stock is counted: the Penn World Table's capital-output ratio of 2.2 is total capital, while the model's 2.36 was private capital on top of public capital of 0.29 of GDP (2.65 in total), which is also why total investment stayed at 33 percent of GDP against the program's 27. Ethiopia's PWT depreciation rate is 5 percent, the OG-Core default, so depreciation was not the gap. The steady-state return on capital rises to about 9 percent — the flip side of a capital stock of 2.2 times GDP with a 30 percent capital share — and household wealth settles near the 2.15 initial-wealth anchor.
* Extended the informality calibration from the steady state to the program and to pensions. The program's revenue gains are direct-tax heavy — in the first nine months of FY2025/26 federal direct taxes grew 78 percent against 41 percent for domestic VAT and 5 percent for import taxes (IMF CR 26/174, p. 15) — so part of the gain is now the tax base broadening rather than higher rates: over the seven program years lifetime-income group 6 moves from half to full income-tax compliance and group 5 from none to a fifth (`labor_income_tax_noncompliance_rate` and `capital_income_tax_noncompliance_rate` are paths, `[1,1,1,1,1,0.5,0]` → `[1,1,1,1,0.8,0,0]`), worth about 0.7 points of GDP of personal income tax, and the remaining gain is split 60/40 between consumption taxes and CIT collections (`tau_c` 0.060 → 0.078, `adjustment_factor_for_cit_receipts` 0.327 → 0.472) instead of two thirds / one third. `replacement_rate_adjust` is set to `[0,0,0,0,0,0.5,1]` by lifetime-income group so that pensions, if a future calibration turns them on, reach only the formal groups that PSSSA and POESSA cover; today the packaged calibration switches the US-style pension formula off (all PIA rates and payments are zero), so no household draws a model pension and the change has no effect on results (pension payouts, about half a percent of GDP, are inside the cash-transfer path).
* Calibrated the capital block to Ethiopia's capital-output ratio and foreign-owned capital stock. The inherited US patience (`beta_annual` 0.96) put the steady-state capital-output ratio near 2.9 and gross investment above 40 percent of GDP, against the Penn World Table's 2.2 and the program's 20–28 percent; and the 4 percent world rate — which in OG-Core fixes the capital stock foreign investors are willing to hold, `K_f = zeta_K (K_open − K_d)` — put the foreign-owned capital stock at 0.37 of GDP against a ~0.24 FDI stock. Two levers, both set to data: `beta_annual` 0.96 → 0.90 (household wealth near 2.4 times GDP, close to the 2.15 initial-wealth anchor) and `world_int_rate_annual` 0.04 → 0.063, UNCTAD's measured return on inward FDI in Africa (World Investment Report 2018). The steady state now has a capital-output ratio of 2.4 (2.1 at the start of the transition), a foreign-owned capital stock of 0.20 of GDP, a return on capital of 6.8 percent and investment of 33 percent of GDP. Patience alone (0.92 with the 4 percent world rate) had taken the capital-output ratio only to 2.6 while pushing the foreign-owned stock up; `households.md` and the world-rate section of `macro.md` document the arithmetic.
* Pinned the project's OG-Core to a build that carries the two unreleased changes this calibration uses — `initial_wealth_ratio` (PSLmodels/OG-Core#1189) and `r_gov_floor` (PSLmodels/OG-Core#1203) — through a `[tool.uv.sources]` entry pointing at `marcelolafleur/OG-Core` branch `integrate/master-plus-1189` (OG-Core master merged into the #1189 branch), so `uv sync` and `examples/run_og_eth.py` reproduce the documented results. The PyPI metadata still says `ogcore>=0.20.0`: a `pip install ogeth` gets the released OG-Core, and the example then starts the transition from steady-state wealth and clips the sovereign rate at zero, saying so. The pin moves back to a PyPI floor once an OG-Core release includes both changes.
* Anchored initial household wealth to the data where the installed OG-Core supports it (PSLmodels/OG-Core#1189, `initial_wealth_ratio`): B(0)/Y = 2.15, the Penn World Table capital-output ratio (2.2) less the foreign-owned capital stock (0.24) plus domestically held public debt (0.19). The example applies it when available and otherwise starts from steady-state wealth, as before; the value moves into the packaged JSON once that OG-Core release is the floor.
* Completed the remittance calibration, following OG-PHL's approach (EAPD-DRB/OG-PHL#85). The level is re-anchored to the observed value: the IMF fifth ECF review (Country Report 26/174, Table 4a) records FY2024/25 net private transfers of USD 7,037 million, 5.6 percent of GDP, so `alpha_RM_1 = alpha_RM_T = 0.056` is now the measured FY2024/25 figure rather than a program anchor (the World Bank WDI series puts the same inflow at USD 7.14 billion). `g_RM` is now a path derived from the packaged `g_n` (`g_RM_t = exp(g_y)(1 + g_n_{t-1}) − 1`) so remittances grow with trend GDP and keep a constant share on the balanced growth path: before `tG1` OG-Core compounds detrended remittances by `(1 + g_RM)/(exp(g_y)(1 + g_n))` regardless of that period's output, and with the shipped scalar `g_RM = 0` Ethiopia's growth rates shrank them relative to trend by about 7 percent a year — from 5.6 to about 1.5 percent of GDP over the first twenty periods — before OG-Core blended them back. Along the baseline transition RM/Y now holds at 5.6–5.7 percent of GDP. `eta_RM` is no longer population-proportional: the World Bank's 2010 national remittance survey shows recipients concentrated in better-off, urban households (recipients' household income 9 percent below ETB 1,000 a month, 47 percent at ETB 1,000–3,500, 37 percent above; 35 percent urban against 16 percent of the population), which maps to remittance value shares by income quintile of 4.8, 4.8, 25.3, 25.3 and 39.8 percent, spread per capita across ages within each lifetime-income group. Both derived objects are rebuilt with the demographics by `update_baseline.py`, and `tests/test_remittances.py` pins the level to its source and asserts the share stays flat along the whole transition.
* Require `ogcore>=0.20.0` (the current OG-Core release), which carries the fixes this calibration depends on: 0.19.1's corrected payroll-tax calculation and bequest split under income-group demographics, and 0.19.2's removal of a payroll-revenue double count that made any model with a nonzero `tau_payroll` fail the steady-state resource constraint. With `tau_payroll = 0.03` the baseline steady state now solves with a resource-constraint error of 2e-14.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-08-31 14:00:00

### Changed
* Require `ogcore>=0.18.0` and migrate the calibration to its income-group-varying demographics (PSLmodels/OG-Core#1165): the packaged demographic arrays (`omega`, `omega_SS`, `rho`, `imm_rates` and their preTP seeds) are regenerated in the new age-by-income shape with the new `update_baseline_demographics` tool (macro parameters untouched, enforced by the tool's clobber guard), and both `get_pop_objs` call sites pass `income_percentiles=p.lambdas.flatten()` as 0.18 requires. OG-ETH's demographics do not vary by income group, so the new arrays are the old ones spread across groups by `lambdas`: the age distribution and the regenerated earnings matrix reproduce the previous values to machine precision, and model results are unchanged. `income.get_e_interp` now reads the OG-USA snapshot's raw JSON values instead of loading them through a `Specifications` object, which decouples it from the installed ogcore's array schema (the 0.18 schema rejects OG-USA's not-yet-migrated shapes) and accepts age weights in either the 1-D or the new age-by-income shape. The multisector JSON's demographic arrays (an older data vintage than the single-industry file) are expanded to the new shape mechanically — distributions scaled by `lambdas`, rates replicated across groups — so their values are bit-for-bit preserved rather than re-downloaded.
* Raised the Python floor to `>=3.12` (matching CI and ogcore's own `>=3.12` requirement; classifiers and the ruff target follow) and relocked to a single `ogcore 0.16.3`, matching OG-ZAF and OG-IDN. This removes the stale Python 3.11 resolution branch that pinned an older ogcore.
* Regenerated the baseline demographics in `ogeth_default_parameters.json` under ogcore 0.16.3, which reworks the pre-time-path population distribution (PSLmodels/OG-Core#1073): the transition-path arrays (`omega`, `g_n`, `imm_rates`, `rho`) shift by one period and three period-0 seeds (`g_n_preTP`, `imm_rates_preTP`, `rho_preTP`) are added.
* Limited the `update_from_api` macro calibration to the sources that are authoritative for Ethiopia: `g_y_annual` (World Bank WDI) and `gamma` (UN ILOSTAT) still update, while the World Bank QPSD debt pull and the IMF `alpha_T`/`alpha_G` pull are switched off (QPSD has no Ethiopia data; the IMF series returns only 2002 values). Debt ratios, `alpha_G`, `alpha_T`, and `r_gov_*` stay at the documented values in `calibration/macro.md`. This refreshes `g_y_annual` (0.060 → 0.0595) and `gamma` (0.518 → 0.517).

### Fixed
* Fixed the demographic `country_id` in `calibrate.py`, which pulled South Africa (UN code 710) data instead of Ethiopia (231), and regenerated the baseline demographics in `ogeth_default_parameters.json`. Steady-state population growth corrects from 0.4% to 2.0%; macro parameters are unchanged.
* Brought all installation instructions in line with the uv workflow the project migrated to in 0.1.0, matching the same fix in OG-PHL and OG-ZAF. The README now documents two supported paths, each as per-platform copy-paste blocks verified end to end: the OG family's universal installer (`install.sh --repo og-eth`, from PSLmodels/OG-Core) and a manual install (install uv, clone, `uv run python examples/run_og_eth.py`). The PyPI install section is dropped: `pip install ogeth` fails outright on the Python that ships with macOS (3.9), silently installs an ogcore older than the tested one on Python 3.11, and does not pin the tested ogcore even on a supported Python. The contributor guide and the UN tutorial no longer instruct readers to build the deleted `ogeth-dev` conda environment (`environment.yml` was removed in 0.1.0, so those steps failed at the first command); both now use `uv sync --extra dev` and `uv run`, the contributor guide's test command matches CI (`pytest -m "not local"` instead of OG-USA's `needs_puf` suite), stale `master`-branch references now say `main`, and the 3-period-model solutions page points at OG-ETH instead of OG-IDN.
